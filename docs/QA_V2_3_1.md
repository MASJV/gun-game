# SHADOW PROTOCOL v2.3.1 — QA pass, functional verification and fixes

A QA pass over v2.3.0, with no new features. Everything below was verified with automation: headless Chromium using
SwiftShader (software WebGL) through Playwright. **No manual play-testing and no real-GPU testing were done.**

## 1. The Hostage Rescue "black screen"

**Reproduced** through the real page (no `?test`), the real menu, the briefing and **DEPLOY**:

- About 4 s after deploying, with no input, the camera had turned about 60° and pitched 45° up (`rotation.x 0.783`).
- It was looking into the night sky. That is near-black on a normal display, with only stars, the moon and the HUD
  visible. The screenshot is the "black screen with nothing to see".

**Root cause.** Clicking DEPLOY requests pointer lock. Chromium then delivers the cursor's warp to the screen centre as
one `mousemove`. The measured event was `movementX -480, movementY -356`. Its order is not fixed: sometimes it arrives
with `pointerLockElement` already set, sometimes just before. Either way, it comes before `pointerlockchange`. The game
treated it as real mouse look.

The V2.3 guard started its ignore-window at `pointerlockchange`, so it never covered that event. The DEPLOY button sits
in the lower half of the briefing card, so the jump always pitched the view upward, into the sky. Hostage Rescue was
not special: every mission start and every **RESUME** could do this. How large the jump was depended on where the
cursor was.

**Fix** (`src/main.js`, `lookGate`):

- Mouse look is ignored from the moment the lock is requested until 80 ms after it is confirmed. The rule also covers
  the plain-lock fallback and lock errors.
- Events are judged by their creation time (`ev.timeStamp`), not by when a slow frame gets around to dispatching them.
  The first e2e matrix showed that under slow frames the warp event can be handled more than 80 ms late.
- The first movement after a lock is dropped if it is an implausible single jump (more than 200 counts). Chrome can
  attach the warp to the user's first real movement.

**Regression coverage** (`tools/e2e.cjs`):

- Every mission start through the real menu must leave the camera level and exactly on the insertion heading.
- A pause → RESUME cycle with the cursor moved away must not turn the view.
- Rendered pixels must show a visible scene.

## 3. Investigated and found to be correct behaviour

- **"Tactical / elite guards never noticed the operator" in early AI soak runs.** This was a test artifact. Guards see
  from their head bone, whose world matrix is refreshed by `renderer.render()` every real frame. Headless simulation
  steps never render, so a guard teleported by the test kept "looking" from his old position. The laser sight hid the
  problem in some runs, because its hit test refreshes nearby soldiers' bones. The soak now refreshes matrices each step,
  as the real loop does.
- **Rounds fired at a guard in COVER did no damage.** He was crouched behind cover, and the rounds stopped in it.
- **The HVT is sometimes not restrainable on the first approach.** He flees when threatened, by design. The e2e walker
  follows and catches him.
- **Doors.** The maps have open doorways, not door objects, so there is nothing to shoot through. Walls, crates,
  containers, vehicles, fences and plates are covered by the ballistics tests.

## 4. What is verified, and how

### Unit and integration suite — `npm test` (`tools/test.cjs`)

The suite now has 121 checks. The 109 from v2.3.0 are unchanged. The new groups are:

- **settings** (6): every Settings control driven through its UI element and checked for its effect:
  - hip, ADS, scope and vertical sensitivity, FOV, volume, camera motion, head bob, weapon sway, sprint FOV
  - performance overlay, adaptive resolution, raw input, low-latency flag, FPS target
  - Low / Medium / High presets: shadows on or off, pixel ratio, shadow-map size, ground clutter, LOD distance
  - storage, restore after a real page reload (model and UI), and reset-to-defaults, which leaves graphics alone
- **loadouts** (2):
  - every weapon × every optic it offers, with every attachment it accepts (18 combinations): the right slot is
    shown and on screen, attachments reach the weapon stats, it aims along the camera axis at full ADS, fires,
    reloads, and switches through every slot and the knife
  - every kit: inventory, frag / flash / smoke throws, medkit and stim healing
- **ai** (1): recruit, tactical and elite, one minute of live contact each:
  - security rises, guards fire, reload, move and never clip into geometry; positions stay finite
  - a guard in the open dies to player fire and the kill is counted
- **robustness** (3): frame-loop failure message, normal play afterwards, WebGL context loss and restore.

### End-to-end — `npm run e2e` (`tools/e2e.cjs`)

Real page, real menu clicks, real briefing, real animation frames, real keyboard input. For each map × mission type ×
supported length, it checks:

1. The briefing and spawn frames render a visible scene. Measured from screenshot pixels with the HUD hidden, so a
   black or sky-only frame fails. The camera is level and on the insertion heading, and the eye height is correct.
2. Pause → RESUME does not turn the view.
3. Holding the real **W** key moves the operator.
4. The mission is played **on foot**, as an objective sequence:
   - the walker follows nav routes with W and mouse-equivalent heading, and jumps at vault and climb links
   - each objective's interaction prompt must appear; E is held for its hold time
   - hostages and targets are freed or restrained and escorted, then it walks to extraction
   - the mission must end **MISSION COMPLETE**

   Guards are counted at spawn, then neutralised, so the run measures mission flow rather than combat luck. Where the
   walker is stuck for more than 4 s, it is moved to the next route point and that is counted as an "assist".
5. REPLAY renders a visible scene again, then RETURN TO MENU, then the next combination, so restarts and map switches
   are exercised.
6. No page errors or console errors.

Results: see section 5.

### Other tools (unchanged, re-run)

- `npm run pace` and `npm run feel`: frame-time and controls measurements.

## 6. Limitations

- Software WebGL only: no real GPU, driver, or display gamma. The original report may also involve a real monitor's
  rendering of a very dark scene. The scene itself is unchanged: night maps stay dark by design.
- Pointer lock is exercised in headless Chromium, which does deliver the warp event. Other browsers, Firefox and
  Safari, were not tested.
- The e2e walker is not a human player. It neutralises guards after counting them and is occasionally assisted past
  tight spots (reported per run).
- Seeds: unit missions use fixed seeds (11, 202, 21, …); e2e uses seed 7 by default (`--seeds` for more). The menu
  itself always rolls a random seed. A 360-start sweep over 12 seeds per combination found no generation or start
  failures.
